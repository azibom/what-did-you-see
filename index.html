The puzzle

Seeing is not the same as receiving an image.

The retinal input can be sparse, interrupted, or ambiguous—and yet a stable object appears in experience.

visible fragments+completion over time→recognized object
03

A useful model

One pass extracts clues.
Later passes refine them.

Feedforward signals carry partial visual information upward. Recurrent signals return to refine, bind, and complete the representation.

Does recurrence equal consciousness?
No. Recurrence supports many computations. The paper tests a perceptual consequence that is consistent with recurrent processing.
04

Why aphantasia?

Imagery and perception may borrow some of the same returning pathways.

Aphantasia is the reported absence of voluntary visual imagery. The authors asked whether its differences extend beyond imagery into perceptual completion.

A difference—not a deficit
The study does not claim that people with aphantasia are less conscious, less creative, or generally worse at seeing.

05

The intervention

A mask does not erase the past.
It interrupts what comes next.

A noise mask arrives before the partial representation has time to stabilize. New feedforward input collides with the ongoing recurrent cycle.

Preserved—but easier to disrupt.
